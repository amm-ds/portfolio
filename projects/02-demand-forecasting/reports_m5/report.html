Demand forecasting on M5

Introduction

This report evaluates demand forecasting on the M5 Walmart subset: 600 daily store-item series (CA_1, CA_3, TX_2), 2011-01-29 → 2016-06-19, 1,105,594 rows, 16.4% zero-demand days — the dense-demand regime where ML forecasting is expected to demonstrably beat seasonal baselines (unlike the sparse UCI Online Retail II track, which stays as the secondary study). Protocol m5_direct_v1: 10 weekly rolling origins (8 selection + 2 scored-once test), horizon 28 days, min training history 1096 days. Candidates: per-series statistical baselines (seasonal_naive, window_avg_28, adida, tsb), automatic per-series models (auto_ets, auto_ces), a global LightGBM with lags/rolling/calendar/exogenous features, and a convex ensemble of the two best ML-family candidates by selection WAPE.

Headline (test origins, scored once)

  • lgbm vs seasonal_naive — test WAPE gap -0.0911 (95% CI [-0.1023, -0.0803], significant); WRMSSE gap -0.1509 (95% CI [-0.1625, -0.1396], significant).
  • convex_ensemble vs seasonal_naive — test WAPE gap -0.1049 (95% CI [-0.1154, -0.0946], significant); WRMSSE gap -0.1574 (95% CI [-0.1679, -0.1470], significant).
  • Test WAPE: naive 0.6032, lgbm 0.5121, ensemble 0.4983.
  • 80% band coverage (split-conformal, selection-calibrated): lgbm 78.0%, ensemble 78.4% (target 80%).

Business insights

  • Forecast value-add: lgbm cuts pooled test WAPE from 0.6032 (seasonal_naive) to 0.5121 — a 15.1% reduction with a 95% paired bootstrap CI on the gap of [-0.1023, -0.0803] units of WAPE (statistically established).
  • Replenishment order quantities: order-up-to policies should read the lgbm point forecast (dense series) instead of the seasonal naive; on intermittent series the gap narrows (dense WAPE 0.4812 vs intermittent 0.6398), so statistical intermittent methods remain competitive there.
  • Safety stock from the band: the split-conformal 80% band is calibrated on selection origins only and covers 78.0% of test actuals — usable as a replenishment buffer width per horizon day.
  • Where ML wins: lgbm beats the naive on 84% of series pooled (85% of dense, 83% of intermittent) — dense high-volume series carry the win.
