CUPED verdict

The adjustment is unbiased by construction — both ATEs are essentially identical and only the variance moves. On conversion it removes 0.08% of variance: prior-year spend barely predicts a ~1% event. On visit, where history is informative, the same machinery removes 0.5%. CUPED is exactly as good as its covariate — covariate selection is the design decision, not the formula.

CUPED, second covariate

Adding recency alongside log_history (multivariate CUPED, additive read under _meta.cuped_multivariate): conversion variance reduction moves from 0.08% to 0.12%, visit from 0.52% to 0.95% — recency adds almost nothing here either. The univariate read above is unchanged; the demo carries both so covariate selection stays measurable, not assumed.

Multi-arm read, honestly stated

This block upgrades the experiment to a multi-treatment read: each arm is scored against the shared control with a cross-fitted T-learner, and a best-arm policy recommends the arm with the highest estimated uplift per customer (no send when both scores are ≤ 0 or tied). Because no randomized assignment realizes the recommended arm mix, every policy-level number here is model-estimated — incremental conversions are the summed predicted uplift over the targeted set, not observed counterfactuals. Only the per-arm Qini/A-B contrasts above are empirical causal reads. Two caveats travel with every figure: predicted magnitudes run hot (the decile check shows ~2x inflation, and the full-base sums include deployment-style scores for the other arm's customers), so the dollar LEVELS are optimistic planning numbers — use them to RANK policies, not to forecast revenue; and the bootstrap resamples rows, not models, so the intervals are within-model precision (the two-arm ranking verdict shows how wide Qini gets across refits).

At the 25% budget the best-arm policy earns $35,514 (model-estimated) vs $29,487 for single-arm Mens targeting and $49,819 for mailing everyone the Mens email. Paired-bootstrap differences (best-arm minus comparator, n=1000, seeded):

  • best_arm - single_arm: [$5,742, $6,347] excludes zero
  • best_arm - random: [$18,619, $19,269] excludes zero
  • best_arm - mail_everyone_mens: [$22,658, $23,466] excludes zero
  • best_arm - mail_everyone_womens: [$29,809, $30,581] excludes zero

Break-even vs mail-everyone-mens: the best-arm policy overtakes full mailing above ≈$0.31 per email (c* = revenue * (inc_mens(1) - inc_best(budget)) / (N * (1 - budget)); incremental conversions are model-estimated (summed predicted uplift)). The paired Qini difference (best-arm - single-arm) is [-12.48, -8.63] and excludes zero. The committed two-arm numbers are unchanged and remain the primary read; this block is the new per-customer channel-choice capability.

Recommended policy

Target the top 25% of customers by t_learner score. Expected incremental profit vs random targeting at that budget: $2,812 per campaign wave over 10 deciles scored out-of-fold.

Predicting churn or conversion is not changing it — see Project 05's fairness finding for the same lesson on the retention side: a response model ranks likely buyers; only an uplift read separates persuadables from sure things and from customers the campaign actively annoys.
