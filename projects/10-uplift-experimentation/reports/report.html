CUPED verdict

The adjustment is unbiased by construction — both ATEs are essentially identical and only the variance moves. On conversion it removes 0.08% of variance: prior-year spend barely predicts a ~1% event. On visit, where history is informative, the same machinery removes 0.5%. CUPED is exactly as good as its covariate — covariate selection is the design decision, not the formula.

Model ranking, with intervals

The response model's Qini area is nominally higher, but the 95% CI on the Qini difference (response - T-learner) [-8.18, 9.52] spans zero — the curve-area ranking is not established; at the 25% budget, the profit difference CI [-3,617, -48] excludes zero — T-learner's budget-profit advantage is established (the individual profit CIs overlap; the paired difference is the honest read). The recommendation stands on the money, and the curve-area ranking is stated with its interval rather than asserted.

Cost sensitivity verdict

Mail-everyone is the profit maximum at the current $0.01 per email. The top-25% policy overtakes it once sends cost more than $0.30 each — the fatigue / list-wear regime — because every marginal email below the cut costs more than the conversions it recovers.

Recommended policy

Target the top 25% of customers by t_learner score. Expected incremental profit vs random targeting at that budget: $2,812 per campaign wave over 10 deciles scored out-of-fold.

Predicting churn or conversion is not changing it — see Project 05's fairness finding for the same lesson on the retention side: a response model ranks likely buyers; only an uplift read separates persuadables from sure things and from customers the campaign actively annoys.
